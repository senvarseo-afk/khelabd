Powerplay Rules in ODIs and T20s: Fielding Restrictions by Over

By KhelaBD editorial · Published Fri 6 Nov 2026 · Updated Fri 6 Nov 2026

A powerplay is a block of overs with a limit on fielders outside the 30-yard circle. In a 50-over match the first 10 overs allow only two fielders outside the circle, overs 11 to 40 allow four, and overs 41 to 50 allow five. In a T20 the first six overs allow two fielders outside the circle and the rest of the innings allows five. When rain shortens an innings the powerplay overs are reduced in proportion. Breaking the limit is a no-ball, which also gives the batting side a free hit.

The six-over powerplay is why T20 openers attack from the first ball and why a scorecard often shows the powerplay score separately; Sportmonks reports it as “pp1” and KhelaBD shows it in the innings summary when available.

When overs are reduced

If an innings is shortened by rain, the powerplay shrinks in proportion. In an ODI cut to 30 overs, powerplay 1 is six overs, powerplay 2 overs 7 to 24 and powerplay 3 overs 25 to 30. In a T20 reduced to 10 overs the powerplay is three overs. The umpires announce the new phases before play restarts; the match page note shows the revised overs.

Other fielding limits that apply all innings

  • No more than five fielders on the leg side at any time.
  • No more than two fielders behind square on the leg side.
  • The wicket-keeper must stay behind the stumps until the ball reaches the batter.

A breach of any of these is a no-ball with a free hit; see the no-ball guide.

Why it matters for Bangladesh

Bangladesh’s T20 problem for most of the last decade has been the powerplay score, not the finish; comparing the pp1 figure with the opponent’s on the match page shows it quickly. In ODIs, the fourth-fielder phase from over 11 to 40 is where spinners such as Mehidy Hasan Miraz do most of their work.
