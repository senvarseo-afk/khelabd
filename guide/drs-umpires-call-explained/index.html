DRS and Umpire's Call Explained: Why 'Hitting the Stumps' Can Still Be Not Out

By KhelaBD editorial · Published Fri 6 Nov 2026 · Updated Tue 10 Nov 2026

The Decision Review System (DRS) lets a team ask the third umpire to check an on-field decision using replays, ball-tracking and edge detection. For lbw, ball-tracking answers three questions in order: where the ball pitched, where it hit the batter, and whether it would have hit the stumps. 'Umpire's call' applies when the evidence is marginal, for example when less than half the ball would have hit the stumps; the on-field decision then stands and the reviewing side keeps its review. Each team gets three unsuccessful reviews per innings in Tests and two in ODIs and T20Is, and a review is lost only when the original decision stands without umpire's call.

What DRS can review

A captain (or the batter who has been given out) can review most decisions: lbw, caught, bowled, stumped, run out and whether a catch carried. The third umpire uses slow-motion replays, the audio-based edge detector (often called Snicko or UltraEdge) and ball-tracking (Hawk-Eye). No-balls are checked by the third umpire automatically on every dismissal and, in international cricket, the front-foot no-ball is checked on every delivery.

The lbw sequence

The third umpire checks three things in a fixed order, and stops as soon as one goes against the appeal:

  1. Pitching. Did the ball pitch in line with the stumps or on the off side? If it pitched outside leg stump, it is not out and nothing else matters.
  2. Impact. Did the ball hit the pad in line with the stumps? If the impact is outside the line of off stump, the batter is only out if they offered no shot.
  3. Wickets. Would the ball have gone on to hit the stumps?

Each check has a tolerance zone. If the evidence is clear, the decision is overturned or confirmed. If it is marginal, the result is umpire’s call.

Umpire’s call

Ball-tracking is a prediction. To allow for its margin of error the ICC defined zones where the on-field umpire’s judgement is kept:

  • For “pitching” and “impact”, umpire’s call applies when the centre of the ball is not inside the line.
  • For “wickets”, umpire’s call applies when less than half the ball would have hit the stumps (the zone covers the outside edges of off and leg stump and the top of the bails).

So “hitting the stumps” on the graphic can still be not out if the on-field umpire said not out, and the same ball can be out if the on-field umpire had given it. That is not the system failing; it is the system deferring to the human when it is not sure. Since 2017 a team does not lose its review when the outcome is umpire’s call.

These limits were raised from two (Tests) and one (ODIs and T20Is) in July 2020 and have been kept in the ICC playing conditions since; the playing-condition changes announced in October 2026 did not include DRS.

A review is lost only when the on-field decision is confirmed, not when it ends in umpire’s call. Reviews do not carry over between innings. The reviewing side must signal within 15 seconds.

Other things fans ask

  • Why was the review “too late”? The 15-second clock runs from the umpire’s decision; if the batter waits for a signal from the dressing room, the review can be refused.
  • Can the umpires review by themselves? The on-field umpires can ask the third umpire about run outs, stumpings, boundaries and catches they could not see, but not about lbw.
  • Does umpire’s call favour the home side? No. The zone is the same for every delivery; it favours the on-field decision, whoever made it.
