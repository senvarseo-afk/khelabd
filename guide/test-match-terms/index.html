Test Match Terms: Sessions, Stumps, Follow-on, Declaration and Draws

By KhelaBD editorial · Published Fri 6 Nov 2026 · Updated Fri 6 Nov 2026

A Test match lasts up to five days with three sessions a day separated by lunch and tea, and each team bats twice. 'Stumps' means the end of a day's play, 'trail by 125' means the batting team is still 125 runs behind the other side's total, a 'follow-on' lets a team that leads by 200 or more make the opponent bat again immediately, and a captain can 'declare' an innings closed to chase a result. A match with no result after five days is a draw.

The day

A Test day is roughly six hours of play in three sessions: morning, afternoon and evening, separated by lunch (40 minutes) and tea (20 minutes). Day-night Tests use a pink ball and shift the breaks later. Ninety overs are scheduled per day; if rain or slow play cuts into them, play can be extended by up to half an hour. Stumps is simply the close of play for the day, which is why our live pages show “Stumps, day 2” rather than a result.

Innings and the match

Each side bats twice, in alternating innings, and the team with more runs across its two innings wins, provided it bowls the other side out twice (or the other side declares). Running out of days before that happens produces a draw, which is not a tie; a tie is when both sides finish on exactly the same total, which has happened only twice in Test history.

Lead and trail

While the second, third or fourth innings is in progress the scorecard shows the difference between the sides. “Bangladesh trail by 125” means Bangladesh’s total so far is 125 short of the opposition’s; “lead by 40” means they are ahead. In the fourth innings the figure becomes a target: the runs needed to win.

Follow-on

If the team batting second finishes its first innings 200 or more runs behind (in a five-day Test), the opposing captain may enforce the follow-on: the trailing team bats again immediately, and if it still cannot pass the lead, the match ends in an innings defeat. Captains often decline the follow-on to rest their bowlers.

Declaration

A captain may declare an innings closed at any time, usually to leave enough time to bowl the opposition out. A scorecard shows a declared innings with a “d”, for example 312/6d. Declaring too late risks a draw; declaring too early risks losing.

Results you will see

  • Won by an innings and 30 runs: the winner needed to bat only once.
  • Won by 7 wickets: the winner reached its fourth-innings target with seven wickets standing.
  • Won by 45 runs: the chasing side was bowled out 45 short.
  • Match drawn: time ran out.
